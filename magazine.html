Our Story

Faith you can measure in small kindnesses.

Al-Ma'un is a word for the small kindnesses — a cup of water, a warm meal, a hand on the shoulder — that a person of faith can never withhold. In the Qur'an, faith is weighed not in grand gestures but in whether you feed the hungry and care for the neighbor at your door.

For more than twenty years that idea has had an address on the Historic Westside. Al-Maun Neighborly Needs is the humanitarian arm of Masjid As-Sabur — the oldest mosque in Las Vegas — and since 2003 it has answered the call the only way it knows how: one neighbor at a time.

We are volunteer-powered and community-led. There's no bureaucracy here, no maze of forms — just neighbors showing up for neighbors, of every faith and none. When Hurricane Katrina displaced thousands, this small congregation earned national recognition for its relief. Muhammad Ali once walked through these doors.

But the everyday work is quieter, and it never stops: a bag of groceries, a clinic visit, a fresh haircut, a second chance. That's the whole mission — the neighbor in front of us, today.
