Our name, our mandate

Faith, measured by how you treat your neighbor

فَوَيْلٌ لِّلْمُصَلِّينَ … وَيَمْنَعُونَ الْمَاعُونَ
"…who are heedless of their prayer — and withhold al-Ma'un, small kindnesses."
Qur'an · Surah Al-Ma'un (107)

We took our name from the chapter of the Qur'an that measures faith by one thing: whether you show up for your neighbor. For more than two decades we've answered that call on the Historic Westside — not with slogans, but with hot meals, wound care, rent paid, and doors that never close.

Al-Maun began in 2003 as the neighborhood service arm of Masjid As-Sabur, the oldest mosque in Las Vegas. It started the way grassroots work always does — a few people, a real need, and no excuse not to help. When Hurricane Katrina hit, that same corner of the Westside organized relief that earned national recognition. Two decades later, it's still neighbors showing up for neighbors.
